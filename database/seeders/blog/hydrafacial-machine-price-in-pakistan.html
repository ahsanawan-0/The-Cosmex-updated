What does 7-in-1, 12-in-1 or 17-in-1 actually mean?

The number is the count of handpieces or functions supplied with the machine. Every hydrafacial machine is built around a hydra-dermabrasion handpiece: a tip that uses vacuum suction to cleanse and exfoliate while it delivers solution to the skin. The extra handpieces add further steps to a facial. The ones you will see most often are:

  • Ultrasonic skin scrubber: a vibrating spatula used to loosen surface debris and help apply products.
  • Radiofrequency (RF) handle: gentle warming used in facial "lifting" steps.
  • Oxygen spray gun: a fine mist of solution or serum.
  • Bio or microcurrent handle: low-level current used in toning steps.
  • Cold hammer: a cooling probe used at the end of a facial.
  • Ultrasound, LED and skin analyser: found on larger systems.

More handpieces means more steps you can offer and charge for, but only if your team is trained to use them. Many clinics find that five or six handpieces do most of the work, so it is worth listing the treatments you plan to sell before you pay for extra functions.

Running costs: what you pay after the machine

The purchase price is only part of the cost. Before you set your treatment prices, add up what each facial uses:

  • Solutions: most protocols use a set of cleansing, exfoliating and hydrating solutions in sequence.
  • Tips: hydra-dermabrasion tips are replaced regularly for hygiene.
  • Filters and tubing: these wear over time and should be changed as the manufacturer recommends.
  • Power protection: a voltage stabiliser or UPS protects the pump and electronics from voltage fluctuations.

A simple way to check whether a machine will pay for itself is: machine price ÷ (price you charge per facial − consumables per facial) = facials needed to break even. As an illustration only, a PKR 185,000 machine, a PKR 5,000 facial and PKR 1,000 of consumables per session would need about 47 facials to cover the machine. Use your own prices and costs for the real figure.

Buying checklist

  1. List the facial steps you will actually sell, then pick the smallest machine that covers them.
  2. Check the suction strength and the pump type, and ask to see the machine working.
  3. Decide whether you need a skin analyser for consultations.
  4. Confirm that replacement tips and solutions are available in Pakistan, and what they cost.
  5. Ask what the warranty covers, for how long, and who repairs the machine.
  6. Ask whether operator training is included at delivery.
  7. Plan for a stabiliser or UPS in the treatment room.
