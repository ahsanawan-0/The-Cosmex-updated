Demand for aesthetic treatments in Pakistan has grown fast, and many doctors, dermatologists and beauty professionals are planning their own clinics. The clinics that do well usually decide their treatment menu first, register properly, and buy the right machines in the right order instead of everything at once.

We supply machines and consumables to clinics across Pakistan, so this guide focuses on what we see every day: which machines to start with, what to budget beyond the machines, and a checklist to work through before opening day. It is general guidance, not legal advice; confirm registration requirements with your provincial regulator.

Step 1: Decide your treatment menu first

Start with the treatments you will sell, then choose machines that deliver them. The most common starting menus combine:

  • Facials: hydrafacial and related skin treatments.
  • Laser hair removal: usually the biggest earner once a clinic is established.
  • Skin tightening: HIFU and RF microneedling.
  • Microneedling and PRP: microneedling pens and a PRP centrifuge.
  • Injectables: doctor-led treatments with prescription products.

Among the most searched aesthetic machines in Pakistan are hydrafacial machines, diode lasers, HIFU and microneedling pens, which is a good indication of what clients are asking for.

Step 2: Registration and qualified staff

Healthcare establishments in Pakistan are regulated by provincial bodies, for example the Punjab Healthcare Commission in Punjab, the Sindh Healthcare Commission in Sindh, the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Health Care Commission and the Islamabad Healthcare Regulatory Authority. Check the registration and licensing requirements for your clinic type before you open.

  • Injectable and prescription treatments must be carried out by qualified doctors. Prescription medicines such as botulinum toxin are regulated by DRAP.
  • Laser and energy-based machines should be operated by trained staff, with eye protection for everyone in the room.
  • Keep written consent, treatment records and your machines' documentation on file.

Step 3: Choose the location and plan the rooms

  • Treatment rooms: plan one room per major machine, especially for lasers.
  • Power: a stable supply, plus a stabiliser or UPS for each machine, protects expensive electronics.
  • Laser room: a door that closes, warning signage and protective eyewear.
  • Clean area: space to prepare and store consumables hygienically.
  • Reception and waiting area: where consultations and first impressions happen.

Step 5: Budget beyond the machines

Machines are usually the largest single cost, but set aside money for:

  • rent, advance and renovation;
  • treatment beds, trolleys, lighting and furniture;
  • consumables for the first months: solutions, cartridges, tips, numbing creams and PRP tubes;
  • staff salaries and training;
  • registration and professional fees;
  • marketing for launch;
  • working capital to cover the first three to six months.

Costs vary widely by city and location, so build your own budget from real quotes rather than averages.

Step 6: Secure your consumables supply

A machine without its consumables is idle. Before you buy, confirm that the tips, cartridges, solutions and spare parts for each machine are available in Pakistan, and keep a small buffer stock. Consumables most clinics need on a regular basis include:

  • Numbing creams for microneedling, laser and PMU procedures.
  • Exosomes and mesotherapy and PDRN solutions.
  • Microneedling cartridges.

Step 7: Training, protocols and safety

Arrange operator training on every machine when it is installed, and write a simple protocol for each treatment: client assessment, consent, settings, aftercare instructions and record keeping. Follow each manufacturer's instructions, and keep a service log for every machine.

Step 8: Get your first clients

  • Create and verify a Google Business Profile with your exact address and phone number.
  • Ask happy clients for Google reviews.
  • Post short videos of your clinic and machines on Instagram and TikTok (with clients' written consent for any before-and-after images).
  • Make booking easy with a WhatsApp number on every page and post.

Opening checklist

  • Treatment menu decided and priced.
  • Registration with the provincial healthcare regulator confirmed.
  • Qualified doctor for injectables and prescription products.
  • Rooms planned, with power protection and laser safety.
  • Machines installed, staff trained, documents filed.
  • Consumables and spare parts in stock.
  • Consent forms, records and aftercare sheets ready.
  • Google Business Profile, social media and WhatsApp booking live.
